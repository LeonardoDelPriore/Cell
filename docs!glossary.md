# CELL Glossary

- **Cell:** on the papers, the unit of the language. It holds executable code, communicates by explicit channels, and is run by the CVM or by CELL'S MACHINE. The printed manual says it is not an entity with its own intelligence. The handwritten notes add a kernel, a stream, dream, and clone. In CELL-0.2 the same word also covers DNA, memory, and adaptation; that wider sense is a proposal, recorded in `docs!vision.md` under "Working definition".
- **Code:** the executable body or portable intermediate representation.
- **DNA:** in CELL-0.2, the executable program representation, including its structure and permitted recombination points. The original papers do not use this word. One sheet calls the executable code genotipo. An earlier glossary line that defined DNA as metadata only is withdrawn.
- **Genotipo:** word used on one original hello-world sheet for the executable code of a cell. Whether it is the same object as DNA is unconfirmed. The sheet is transcribed in `docs/Documentazione originale/trascrizioni/IMG_20261013_203856001_HDR.md`.
- **CVM:** Cell Virtual Machine in the printed manual: the opcode interpreter, meta-opcode interpreter, anomaly manager, thread interpreter, and coordinator. On 15 November 2026 Leonardo also called it the environment. It compares groups of identical cells, drops cells whose outputs disagree with an agreeing group, and gives fitness priority to the tissue or set of tissues with greater weight, as in a pyramid. A weight is an opcode function, not a constant. On 17 November 2026 the ranking measure of a mutation under test was stated. The kernel tests a sample by level against the dream accumulated there. Significant mutations hardly occur until that dream is sufficient. Groups that win 90% or 100% of the results can then be sent to test and mutated again. True fitness is less time at equal results, and the same outputs for the same input. Growth follows input. On 17 November 2026 a stimolo was defined as an input. The more input a cell receives, the faster the CVM reproduces it; if the environment saturates, the CVM pushes it to seek other places to live. More stimuli on an area, more the network develops there. The CVM also includes a human-only editor for designing the Cell language and for viewing the network.
- **Editor:** author instrument, 15 November 2026, inside the CVM, for a human only. The human designs the Cell language there and navigates the network schemas in three dimensions: where the network is resilient, and where its functions are. The human may color areas. Selecting an area shows that dream and its data. The programmer may edit cell opcode from this side. A specialized AI may edit cell opcode as the programmer may. The editor itself is not that AI's instrument. The papers do not describe an editor.
- **Mitosi:** in the printed manual, creation of a cell that contains the same executable code as the mother cell.
- **Dream:** in the handwritten notes, a dynamic memory structure used to search, test, and store input/output associations in the background. A public dream is visible to the processes of one group. On 15 November 2026 Leonardo called the cell's dream area the cytoplasm. Later the same day, every level of tissues has its own dream, and each dream is three things together: the neural network built at that level, the memory, and the response time. First answers of a cell come from the opcode or genotipo. Later answers come from dream and become faster, until a subdivision. Dream also crosses groups. The papers do not use the words cytoplasm, tissue, or macro-group.
- **Citoplasma:** author name, 15 November 2026, for the dream area of one cell. Not a word from the papers. See Dream.
- **Tessuto:** author name, 15 November 2026, for cells taken together, as a name for a human reader. Tissues can contain tissues, and each level has its own dream. Many tissue functions, together with the inter-tissue functions that connect them, form an organ. Separate cells may also live inside higher groups. The names do not limit CELL to an earthly organism. The manual already names a cellular group, extension `.CELG`. The two names are not yet declared identical. A tissue keeps working if its validated cells live on different machines. His example is one hundred cells on one hundred machines.
- **Intertessuto:** author name, 15 November 2026, for a connection between tissues. Together with tissue functions, inter-tissue functions form an organ. The papers do not use the word.
- **Organo:** author name, 15 November 2026, for the union of many tissue functions and many inter-tissue functions, as a name for a human reader. It has its own dream. Separate cells may live at this level. The papers do not use the word.
- **Individuo:** author name, 15 November 2026, for a set of many organs, as a name for a human reader. It has its own dream. He does not declare it the last level: every further abstraction still has a dream attached. Separate cells may live at this level. The papers do not use the word.
- **Macrogruppo:** author name, 15 November 2026, for a group of groups in the macro-network. Dream crosses these groups. The papers do not use the word.
- **Suddivisione:** author word, 15 November 2026, for the split that ends the maturation from mechanical answers to dream answers. The manual's mitosi is the nearest historical operation. They are not yet declared identical.
- **Stream:** in the handwritten notes, the data container of a cell. Several sheets treat it as the only data type.
- **Stimolo:** author definition, 17 November 2026: an input. The quorum compares cells that receive the same input. Growth follows input: more input on an area, more the network develops there. The papers already use the word. The manual says a cell sends or receives a stimulus. One printed page says stimuli represent both normal situations and error situations. Those sentences stay as written. The definition today is input.
- **Kernel:** in the handwritten notes, the nucleus of a cell that holds code and coordinates input, dream, and output. On 15 November 2026 Leonardo called it a laboratory: a mutation can be introduced there, and it wins if it responds better than the previous copy. On 17 November 2026 he said how that test works. The kernel tests a sample by level, against the dream accumulated there. A system at the start hardly has significant mutations. When dream is sufficient, groups that win 90% or 100% of the results can be sent to test and mutated again. The mutation that wins returns the same outputs for the same inputs and, among those results, takes less time.
- **VITA:** in CELL'S MACHINE, the process that stays active and can manage other processes.
- **Opcode dinamico:** an instruction removed after it executes.
- **Opcode statico:** an instruction that remains for the next run.
- **Prologatta:** word written on one handwritten sheet for a proposed language layer above the cell, drawn above a linker, a virtual motor, and execution. It is not a term of the printed manual. Whether it means Prolog is unconfirmed. The sheet is transcribed in `docs/Documentazione originale/trascrizioni/IMG_20261013_204209593_HDR.md`.
- **Memory:** state or experience retained by a cell under an explicit policy.
- **NeuralCore:** the neural network built inside a dream. On 15 November 2026 Leonardo placed it there, at every tissue level, together with memory and response time. It does not assign fitness. Fitness stays with the environment. It does not place work. Liquidity does that. Its weights start defined and can change. Each weight is an opcode function, not a constant number.
- **Peso:** author term, 15 November 2026, for a weight inside that network. It is an opcode function with an initial definition, able to change. The printed manual's constant function is a different object: there, a variable is constant. The environment uses greater weight to prioritize tissues. Which function the test keeps was stated on 17 November 2026: same outputs for the same input, then less time. The winning opcode is the one the test keeps.
- **Liquidità:** author term, 15 November 2026. Any computation written in Cell can live on any connected machine able to compute it. The network weighs itself and redistributes toward an equilibrium whose criterion is resilience, not speed. This is not the same term as Liquid Software (Hartman, Manber, Peterson, and Proebsting, 1996), which moves code through a network and does not define a cell, dream, or a resilience equilibrium.
- **Fitness:** author measure, 17 November 2026, for a mutation under test. True fitness is time: at equal results the higher fitness is less time, and at equal input the outputs are the same. The kernel tests a sample by level, against the dream accumulated at that level. Significant mutations hardly occur at the start. When dream is sufficient, groups that win 90% or 100% of the results can be sent to test and mutated again. He said those two rates with "magari". What counts as sufficient dream, and which clock measures the time, are unsaid. This measure ranks the mutation. Placement across machines stays the resilience equilibrium of 15 November 2026. The papers do not define this function.
- **Evolution:** the controlled creation and evaluation of candidate cell variants. On 17 November 2026 the evaluation measure is fitness: same outputs for the same input, then less time.
- **Migration:** moving a cell, or the work it is doing, onto another connected machine. The 15 November 2026 decision makes this liquidity: the reason to move is resilience, not speed. A checkpoint is one proposed way to move a running calculation without losing it.
- **Peer:** a runtime node that can communicate directly with other participating nodes.
- **Checkpoint:** a portable snapshot sufficient to resume a supported cell at a defined point.
- **Fencing:** a mechanism that prevents a former owner from continuing to modify state after ownership has moved.
